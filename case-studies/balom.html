Challenge

Mothers lose themselves in the process of caring for everyone else.

When mothers gain full responsibility for another human being overnight, they often begin to neglect themselves — mentally, physically, and emotionally. The challenge was to design an app that empowers mothers to prioritize their own wellbeing without adding to their already-overwhelming to-do list.

💪
Mothers need empowerment — not another obligation
🪞
Difficult to identify when self-neglect is happening
⚡
Lack of motivation after a full day of caregiving
⚖️
Always busy — never busy doing something for themselves
Research

Listen first. Design second.

Rather than assuming what mothers needed, I ran a multi-phase research program to understand their real behavior, motivations, and frustrations before touching a single wireframe.

📋
Screener Survey
Recruited mothers aged 18–40 with children under 5 who own a smartphone. Finding the right participants was critical.
📔
3-Day Diary Study
Mothers tracked every app they used for 3 days — revealing natural behavior patterns without observation bias.
🎙
User Interviews
3 in-depth interviews over FaceTime and Zoom to understand the "why" behind their most-used apps — especially organizational ones.
💡
Most-used apps beyond social media: organizational, health, and e-commerce — mothers use apps for accountability and the feeling of accomplishment when checking something off.
💡
Mothers use organizational apps as reminders and accountability tools — the dopamine hit of completing a task was a key motivator.
💡
Three user types emerged: the stay-at-home mother who needs motivation, the working mother who needs prioritization, and the expecting mother who needs balance.

Reframing the problem

With research complete, I distilled the core frustrations — lack of prioritization, motivation, and balance — into actionable "How Might We" questions to guide ideation.

"How might we help mothers prioritize their overall health?"
"How might we help mothers find a balance between caring for themselves and their families?"
"How might we support mothers through postpartum?"
"How might we increase productivity and organization in a mother's daily routine?"
Design

Gamification as the core mechanic

After competitive analysis of Google Keep, Monday.com, and Cozi — and finding no app specifically serving this audience — I landed on the idea that leverages what the research revealed: mothers respond to accountability and the feeling of accomplishment.

🎮

Gamification layer

Designed to encourage dopamine release through visual positive reinforcement. Progress bars, streaks, and completion animations make self-care feel rewarding — not obligatory.

💆

"Self" section

A dedicated section acts as a constant visual cue for self-care — separate from tasks and goals so it never gets buried.

✅

Checklist-style goals

Tasks organized in a checklist format make goals feel attainable — especially for mothers who feel overwhelmed by the sheer volume of things to manage.

👥

Community feed

Mothers can share goals publicly and interact with others — adding social accountability that the research showed was a key motivator.

Testing & Iteration

Two rounds. Real feedback. Better design.

I conducted 5 usability tests via Zoom — tasking participants with navigating the home, analysis, calendar, and goals pages. The feedback drove a full redesign of the dashboard.

📝
Homepage felt overwhelming — simplified the information hierarchy and reduced cognitive load
📝
Labels weren't intuitive (especially "Analysis") — renamed and reorganized the navigation
📝
Needed more gamification — added streaks, progress rings, and animated completion states
View interactive prototype in Figma
Impact
What Balom made possible
Defined 3 distinct user archetypes through rigorous research — shaping every design decision
Identified gamification as the core differentiator — an insight that came entirely from research, not assumption
Two rounds of usability testing validated the concept and improved task completion and learnability
Delivered a high-fidelity, interactive Figma prototype ready for development handoff
Reflection

Research as the foundation of everything

I was excited to take on a project centered on solving real problems for mothers. Balom felt like the right opportunity to deepen my user research and interview skills — especially because I could personally connect with the subject matter.

The most important lesson: gamification wasn't something I invented for this app. It's what the research told me mothers already responded to. That's the difference between designing for users and designing with them.

Special thanks to the usability testers who made this project stronger: Jennifer On, Christina Phuong, Mariah Matthews, Taylor King, Marissa Roberson, Brea Thompson, and Kyra Grant.
